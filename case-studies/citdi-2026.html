Research · Peer-Reviewed Paper

User Sentiment Patterns and Satisfaction Drivers in Mobile App Ecosystems

Accepted — CITDI 2026Springer / EAIScopus-indexed10,303 reviews

Overview

My first peer-reviewed publication, accepted at CITDI 2026 (Springer/EAI, Scopus-indexed). The paper asks a deceptively simple question: what actually drives user satisfaction in mobile apps, versus what app teams assume drives it?

Method

  • Data: 10,303 Google Play Store reviews collected across a range of app categories.
  • Statistical analysis: Mann-Whitney U tests and Chi-square tests to identify meaningful differences in sentiment across app segments.
  • Machine learning: Supervised classifiers trained to predict satisfaction drivers from review text and metadata.

Post-acceptance work

Since acceptance, I've been running an ablation study and additional cross-validation passes ahead of the camera-ready submission, and preparing the formal AI-use disclosure required by Springer's editorial policy.

Why it matters

This paper is the first formal publication credential on my PhD pathway, and it sits at the intersection of my two tracks — using rigorous statistical methods (research) to answer a genuinely useful product question (design).
