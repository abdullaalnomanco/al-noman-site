Research · Final Major Project (FGCT7024)

Modeling Trust Calibration in Human-AI Financial Decision Systems

In Progress — FMPUCA Farnhamn = 678 (6,780 obs.)

Overview

This is my Final Major Project for the MSc in Human-Computer Interaction at the University for the Creative Arts, Farnham, supervised by Roderick Morgan. It investigates how people calibrate trust in AI-generated credit recommendations — specifically whether they end up over-trusting, under-trusting, or reaching an appropriately calibrated level of reliance.

The central question: does explaining an AI decision actually help people trust it the right amount, or does it just make them trust it more regardless of whether that trust is deserved?

Method

  • Design: A React-based experimental stimulus presenting 10 fictional loan scenarios under three explanation conditions — none, surface-level, and counterfactual.
  • Measurement: The Jian et al. (2000) Trust in Automation scale, paired with reflexive thematic analysis (Braun & Clarke, 2019) of participant reasoning.
  • Data: A synthetic dataset of 678 respondents (6,780 observations in long-form) built to pilot the analysis pipeline ahead of the live study.

Where it stands

Following supervisor feedback that the original proposal "looked like an experiment without substantial development," I added a second phase: designing and evaluating an explanation-interface tool based on the study's own findings. That extra "Making" component is what pushes this from a pure research study into a proper HCI design contribution.

Why it matters

Explainable AI is often built on the assumption that more explanation is always better. This project tests that assumption directly, in a domain — financial decision-making — where miscalibrated trust has real consequences.
