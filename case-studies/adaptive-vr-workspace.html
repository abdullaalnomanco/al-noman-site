Research · UCA Coursework Project (FGCT7023)

Adaptive VR Workspace

8.25/10 ProductivityFigma prototypePostgrad cohort study

Overview

A VR workspace system that adapts to how the user is actually feeling, not just what they're doing. It offers four presets — Focus, Calm, Energy, and Social — that respond to a user's mood, focus level, and energy.

Theoretical grounding

The design draws on Attention Restoration Theory, Construal Level Theory, Affective Computing, Norman's Emotional Design framework, Self-Determination Theory, the Yerkes-Dodson law, and biophilic design principles — treating the environment itself as a design material, not just a backdrop.

Testing & findings

  • Tested with 2 UCA postgraduate design students; mean perceived productivity of 8.25/10.
  • The clearest preference to emerge: users wanted a persistent, floating environment switcher — a "chat-head" style control — rather than a menu they had to leave the experience to access. That maps directly onto calm-technology principles: the control should be always-available but never demanding attention.

Prototype

Built as a full interactive Figma prototype (file key iJsowJHWLi2JGhp6bvJ6wH), covering all four environment presets and the switcher interaction.
